Research questions that start in the field.

Understanding mechanisms. Testing whether they matter.

Borderbio is interested in research at the interface between agricultural amendments, soil biology, nutrient availability and crop performance.

Plant-derived amendments

A central area of interest is the use of plant-derived compounds and extracts as agricultural amendments. We investigate how their chemistry changes during preparation and application, and whether those compounds can influence soil microbial communities, nutrient availability and crop response.

Testing agricultural amendments

The same experimental approach can be applied to other agricultural amendments, including biological products, fertilisers and soil conditioners. By following effects from soil biology through nutrient availability to crop response, we can investigate not simply whether a treatment works, but how it works.

Soil microbiome and function

We are particularly interested in how treatments alter microbial communities and functional groups within soil, and whether those biological changes can be linked to measurable changes in nutrient cycling and plant performance.

From controlled experiments to the field

Promising responses can be investigated first under controlled conditions and then taken into replicated field trials. This allows us to ask whether an effect that is measurable in the laboratory or pot experiment remains relevant under real agricultural conditions.
