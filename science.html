Science Research About Work With Us

Following what happens in the soil.

From amendment to biological response.

Borderbio investigates how agricultural amendments influence soil biology, nutrient availability and crop response.

Plant chemistry

Plants produce a wide range of natural compounds involved in signalling, defence and responses to stress. Borderbio extracts and profiles these compounds to understand what is present and how different treatments alter the chemical profile of plant material.

This provides the first step in linking plant-derived chemistry with changes in soil biology and, ultimately, crop response.

Soil biology

We assess how agricultural amendments including plant extracts alter microbial communities in soil, using molecular tools to detect changes in both the organisms present and even the genes associated with important soil functions.

Depending on the question, this can include PCR for particular microbial groups or functional genes, as well as DNA sequencing to describe broader changes in microbial diversity.

Available nutrients

Changes in soil biology only become agronomically interesting if they influence what happens in the soil. We therefore measure soil chemistry alongside biological responses to determine whether an amendment changes the availability of nutrients to the crop.

By following biology and nutrient availability together, we can begin to define measurable biological responses that may have practical agricultural significance.

Crop response

Ultimately, a change in soil chemistry or biology needs to translate into something useful to agriculture. We use controlled plant experiments to determine whether treatments affect crop growth, health or performance.

Promising effects can then be taken from controlled experiments into replicated field trials, allowing us to test whether the response persists under real-world agricultural conditions.
